# STYLE BIBLE — ANCIENTS (rift)

**Status: FROZEN.** Embedded verbatim in every visual implementer's brief. No implementer may
contradict it. If a rule here is unbuildable, the task fails back to the orchestrator with the
contradiction cited — nobody reinterprets it locally.

This bible **supersedes** the flat-shaded material law in `games/rift/CONTRACT.md` §6/§7. That
amendment is recorded formally in `games/rift/GRAPHICS_CONTRACT.md` §1. Everything in RIFT's
contract that this bible does not explicitly overturn still binds.

---

## 0. The benchmark

**Dota 2 (Valve).** Every capture this build produces is shown to a harsh, context-free critic in an
unlabeled A/B pair against a real Dota 2 screenshot of the same shot type. The bar is **wins or
ties**. "Good for a browser game" is a failing grade. Reference shots live in `judge/reference/`.

What we are actually chasing from Dota 2 — in priority order, because these are what a viewer reads
in the first half-second:

1. **Terrain that is a place, not a plane.** Elevation, cliffs with rock faces, a river cutting the
   map, tree-walled jungle. Dota's map reads as carved landscape; ours currently reads as a table.
2. **Surface variety under one light.** Mud, wet stone, mossy rock, worn gold, dry grass — all
   plausibly lit by the same sun because they differ in *roughness*, not just hue.
3. **Ambient occlusion everywhere.** Contact darkening where anything meets the ground, in crevices,
   under canopies. This single cue does more for "shipped" than polygon count.
4. **Silhouette hierarchy.** Heroes read instantly as individuals; creeps read as a mass; structures
   read as landmarks. Never confusable at gameplay zoom.
5. **FX with weight.** Abilities have anticipation, a bright core, a colored bloom, ground decals,
   and debris. Not a burst of octahedra.

What we deliberately do **not** chase: photoreal skin/cloth, hand-painted texture art, skeletal
animation, licensed-quality hero designs. We have no artists and no asset pipeline. Every surface in
this game is generated by code. Our win condition is **art direction and lighting**, not asset
fidelity — a strongly art-directed procedural world beats a weakly-directed textured one, and that is
the gap we are exploiting.

## 1. Mood

**Overgrown celestial ruins at dusk, and the same ruins under a cold moon.**

Two ancient powers wage a small war across a valley their builders abandoned. Stone monuments the
size of temples stand half-swallowed by moss and root. A shallow river runs the diagonal, catching
the last warm light. On the high ground the rock is bare and wind-scoured; in the low jungle it is
wet, dark and close.

The world is **cool, damp and dark**; the two teams are the only saturated warm/cold lights in it.
Anything that matters to gameplay — a hero, an ability, a structure's team crystal, a gold bounty —
is *brighter and more saturated than the world it stands on, always*. That is the whole readability
strategy: the world is a dim stage and the gameplay is lit.

Touchstones beyond Dota 2: the wet-stone-and-moss lighting of *Journey*'s ruins; the silhouette
discipline of *Bad North*; the vertical-relief legibility of *Ori*'s environments.

## 2. Material model — PBR (amended, replaces the flat-Lambert law)

**One model, applied to everything. No mixing.**

- **`MeshStandardMaterial` for every world and unit surface.** `MeshBasicMaterial` /
  `MeshLambertMaterial` / `MeshPhongMaterial` are **banned** on anything the sun touches.
- **Smooth shading by default.** `flatShading: true` is permitted only where the form is genuinely
  faceted — cut stone, crystals, cliff rock. Organic and manufactured curved surfaces shade smooth.
- **IBL is mandatory.** PBR with no environment map renders as plastic; this is the single most
  common way a PBR conversion looks *worse* than the flat-shaded version it replaced. Every material
  is lit by a `PMREMGenerator`-processed environment built from our own procedural sky scene, applied
  as `scene.environment`. It is regenerated when the time of day changes. **A build where
  `scene.environment` is null is a failed build**, not a tuning issue.
- **Every texture is generated in code.** No image files, no `TextureLoader`, no fetched assets. The
  frozen kit provides the generators; implementers call them. Bundle stays asset-free.
- **Every material carries at minimum:** albedo (from the palette), a roughness map or a roughness
  constant, and a normal map where the surface has structure. Metalness is 0 for everything except
  the explicitly metal families.
- **Post-processing is mandatory and its stack is fixed** (see §6).

### The frozen surface table

All materials are constructed through **one** factory, `surface(id)`, from the frozen kit. Nobody
calls `new THREE.MeshStandardMaterial` directly. Each `SurfaceId` binds an albedo palette key plus
its physical parameters; the exact table is Layer-1 data in `games/rift/shared/src/surfaces.ts`.
Roughness is the primary storytelling dial — it is what makes wet river stone read differently from
dry high-ground rock under the same sun.

| family | reads as | roughness | metalness | normal | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `groundMoss` | damp open ground | 0.95 | 0 | fine noise | the darkest large surface |
| `groundDirt` | worn lane earth | 0.90 | 0 | coarse | lane edges, camp floors |
| `lanePaving` | worn flagstone | 0.72 | 0 | slab-seam | lanes read as *built* |
| `cliffRock` | bare wind-scoured rock | 0.85 | 0 | strong strata | flat-shaded, faceted |
| `wetRock` | river-washed stone | 0.35 | 0 | medium | low roughness = wet |
| `riverWater` | shallow moving water | 0.08 | 0 | scrolling ripple | transparent, animated |
| `monumentStone` | tower / ancient body | 0.68 | 0 | carved detail | lighter than lane stone |
| `bark` | trunk | 0.92 | 0 | vertical strata | |
| `canopy` | leaf mass | 0.88 | 0 | soft | subtle translucency fake |
| `fern` | undergrowth | 0.85 | 0 | soft | |
| `cloth` | banners, robes, tabards | 0.95 | 0 | weave | team colored |
| `leather` | armor straps, packs | 0.75 | 0 | grain | |
| `iron` | weapons, armor plate | 0.45 | 1.0 | brushed | true metal |
| `bronze` | trim, braziers, ornament | 0.35 | 1.0 | hammered | true metal, warm |
| `gold` | bounty, ancient ornament | 0.25 | 1.0 | polished | true metal, the treasure read |
| `crystal` | team crystals, ancient heart | 0.10 | 0 | none | emissive; **not** transmissive — the frozen factory returns `MeshStandardMaterial`, which has no transmission. The read is emissive core + bloom, not refraction. |

Two metal families and a real gold are non-negotiable: **a PBR scene with no metal in it has no
reason to be PBR.** Metal catching the sun is what sells the material model at a glance.

## 3. Palette

**All color continues to come from the frozen `APAL` palette** in
`games/rift/shared/src/palette.ts`. Ad-hoc hex in implementer code remains a contract violation, and
reviewers check for it. The only sanctioned derivations remain `mix()` and `composite()` from
`@platform/shared` with **both endpoints** palette entries.

The palette is **extended** (never re-valued) for this pass with the new terrain, jungle, metal and
night families; the extension and its ladder proofs are specified in `GRAPHICS_CONTRACT.md` §3.
Existing entries keep their exact values, so every existing ladder assertion still holds.

The **value ladder law** (`valueLadder.test.ts`) continues to bind unchanged, and its thresholds may
not be weakened. It is *extended* with cases for the new families. Note what it now means: the ladder
governs **albedo** separation, and under PBR the *rendered* value also moves with roughness and
occlusion. Albedo separation is therefore a floor, not a guarantee — which is exactly why the
rendered-pixel judge loop in Phase 4 exists and is the real gate.

## 4. Lighting recipe

One recipe, two states. Both states must look deliberate; night is not "day with the lights off."

**Shared rig:**
- **Environment (IBL):** procedural PMREM from the sky scene. This is the fill light; there is no
  separate hemisphere light doing the fill's job any more.

  **Environment scene recipe (frozen).** The PMREM source scene contains: (a) a small
  high-intensity sun/moon disc at `emissiveIntensity` 6.0 day / 1.5 night — this is the specular
  anchor and the reason metal reads as metal; (b) the three-band vertical gradient with real value
  falloff, authored in HDR (values above 1) even though every hue still resolves to `APAL`; (c) a
  ground hemisphere at low albedo for the bounce term. PMREM at 256², regenerated only on
  `setTimeOfDay`, never per frame.
- **Sun / moon:** one `DirectionalLight`, `castShadow`, shadow map **4096**, and — critically — its
  shadow frustum is fitted to **the camera's visible ground footprint, not the whole map**. Fitting
  to the map is what makes a 2048 map look soft and muddy at close zoom; fitting to the view gives
  us close-up shadow detail for free. The frustum must be snapped to texel increments so shadows do
  not swim as the camera pans.
- **Bounce:** the IBL owns ambient fill entirely. The existing `HemisphereLight` is **removed** — a
  hemisphere light on top of an environment map double-counts the fill and is what flattens a PBR
  scene back into looking Lambert.
- **Fog:** `FogExp2` in the state's fog color. **S2 still binds: fog color equals the horizon stop,
  exactly.** Terrain must never blend into the sky (S4).

**DAY (default).** Low warm sun, elevation ~28°, azimuth 225°, long raking shadows across the lanes.
Sky: warm horizon into a cool deep zenith (S1 binds — zenith cooler and ≥12 L\* darker). The mood is
the last half-hour of usable light. Exposure tuned so the moss ground sits low but never crushes:
the ground is the darkest large surface but it must stay *readable* as a surface, with its normal
detail visible.

**NIGHT.** Cold, high, dim moon (elevation ~62°, blue-white), much weaker directional intensity but
*harder* shadows. Environment noticeably dimmer and bluer. Fog denser. The consequence, which is the
whole point: **the team lights, ability FX, brazier fires and the ancients' hearts become the primary
light sources in the frame.** Night is when this art direction is at its strongest — a dark world
with a handful of saturated lights is exactly what our constraints are good at, and it is where we
have the best chance against Dota 2's night shots.

Emissive surfaces (crystals, braziers, hearts, FX cores) drive selective bloom in both states, and
are tuned so they are *pleasant* by day and *dominant* by night.

## 5. Camera & framing

Unchanged where it works: fixed pitch **55°**, fixed yaw, perspective FOV **50**, default height
**36**. Do not "improve" the camera into an orbit cam — the fixed MOBA framing is correct and every
capture depends on it being stable.

**One measured change: the minimum height drops from 18 to 11.** Verified against a baseline capture
at full zoom-in — a hero occupies roughly 40 px of a 1080p frame at camH 18. At that size a 45–70
part hero silhouette is *entirely wasted effort*: no headgear, cape, weapon or animation is legible,
and the close-up shots the judge compares against Dota 2 hero shots cannot be won. Every argument in
§7 for hero detail is void unless the camera can actually get close enough to see it. `CAM_MIN_H`
becomes **11** (R_WIRE owns `game.ts`); `CAM_MAX_H` and the default are unchanged, so normal play
framing does not move.

What changes: the camera now looks at terrain with real height, so the **ground plane under the
camera target must be height-sampled** — the camera rides the terrain rather than a flat y=0, or the
framing will drift as the player pans onto high ground.

Framing discipline for every shot the judge sees: the horizon line must sit in the upper third or be
absent entirely; the frame must never be more than ~60% empty ground; there must be at least one
strong vertical (tree, cliff, tower) breaking the horizontal banding of the lanes.

## 6. Post-processing stack (fixed order, mandatory)

`EffectComposer`, in exactly this order:

1. **`RenderPass`**
2. **Ambient occlusion** — screen-space (GTAO preferred, SSAO acceptable). Radius tuned to contact
   scale, not scene scale: we want darkening in the centimetres-to-a-metre range where units meet
   ground and where rocks meet rocks. Strength must survive a side-by-side "AO on/off" check; if you
   cannot see the difference, it is mistuned.
3. **Selective bloom** — **layer-masked, not threshold-masked**: the composer renders only
   `BLOOM_LAYER` into a second target and combines it additively. A global luminance threshold
   cannot separate an emissive crystal from sun-lit metal and must not be used. Team crystals,
   braziers, ability cores, the ancients' hearts, gold. Radius moderate, strength restrained. Bloom
   on everything is the classic amateur tell; bloom on exactly the light sources is what makes night
   work.
4. **Color grade + vignette** — a subtle filmic curve pushing shadows cool and highlights warm, plus
   a gentle vignette. This is what unifies the frame into "one photograph."
5. **`OutputPass`** (tone mapping + sRGB) then **SMAA/FXAA** — the composer defeats MSAA, so
   antialiasing must be reintroduced here or every edge in the game will crawl.
   The operator is **`THREE.NeutralToneMapping`, not ACES** — a round-4 art-judge amendment already
   recorded in `scene.ts`: ACES compresses mid-tones and shifts hue, so sun-lit moss measured L\*≈8
   against a palette L\*≈22 at any exposure. **Do not re-litigate this.** R_SCENE owns
   `renderer.toneMapping` and `renderer.toneMappingExposure`; `OutputPass` inherits both from the
   renderer and R_POST sets neither. Exposure is `2.75` at full day and is changed by exactly one
   thing — `setTimeOfDay`, which ramps it to `1.9` at full night.

Disabling any pass "for test performance" is a **banned** regression — it is the documented way this
kind of build quietly gets ugly. If a capture run is too slow, reduce capture resolution, never the
stack.

## 7. Silhouette language

Readable at gameplay zoom (camH 36) from the top-down 55° angle — which means **silhouettes must
work from above**, where a humanoid's most distinctive feature (its face) is invisible. Shoulders,
headgear, weapons held out from the body, capes, and ground shadow do the work.

- **Heroes — 1.9u, and every hero must be identifiable in pure black silhouette from directly
  above.** This is the single hardest requirement in this document and the current build fails it
  outright: six heroes share one rig with a bolt-on prop. Each hero now gets its own **distinct
  proportion set** (shoulder width, height, stance, mass distribution) *plus* at least two
  **silhouette-breaking elements** that extend beyond the body envelope — a cape, a back-mounted
  weapon, a horned or crowned headpiece, a shoulder banner, floating orbits, a cloak spread. Part
  budget: **45–70 per hero.** They are the closest thing to the camera and carry the whole build.
- **Creeps — 1.5u, a legible mass.** Three types must differ at a glance by *body shape*, not by
  color: melee is a compact wedge behind a shield; ranged is a tall thin robed spindle; siege is a
  wide low quadruped hauling a ram. Part budget **22–35**. They read as a *wave* — that is a
  composition requirement, so their spacing and slight rank variation matter as much as the mesh.
- **Neutral camp creeps — deliberately non-humanoid.** Beasts, not soldiers: a hulking bear-form, a
  pack of low quadrupeds, an insectile hive. They must never be mistaken for a lane creep at any
  zoom. Part budget **25–40**.
- **Towers — landmarks, 9u.** Tapered stone shaft on a wide plinth, a carved cornice, a burning
  brazier, a slowly turning team crystal at the crown, banners on the windward side, and *visible
  damage state*. Part budget **55–80**.
- **Ancients — the biggest silhouette in the game, 14u.** A kneeling colossus of cracked monument
  stone around a suspended crystal heart, on a tiered dais with a gold pool. Visible from across the
  map and unmistakable. Part budget **110–160**.
- **Trees — the jungle's walls.** Not lollipops. Trunk with root flare, 3–5 branch levels, layered
  canopy shells of varying scale and rotation, some leaning, some dead and bare. **Minimum 6 distinct
  tree archetypes**, each with seeded per-instance variation in scale, rotation, lean and canopy
  fullness so no two adjacent trees are visibly the same object. Part budget **18–40** each.

Nothing anywhere may be thin enough to alias at gameplay zoom.

## 8. World population

The single most damning line in the audit of the current build was that the world is three prop
archetypes copy-pasted. That is the thing to kill.

**Density targets** (per 100 m² of the relevant zone, seeded and deterministic):

- **Jungle zones:** 8–14 trees, 20–35 undergrowth clusters (ferns, bushes, saplings), 6–12 rocks,
  2–5 fallen logs / stumps, 1–3 ruin fragments. Jungle must feel *enclosing* — the tree line is a
  wall you cannot see through, because that is what makes a MOBA jungle legible as a space.
- **Open ground / lane shoulders:** 3–6 grass tufts, 2–4 rocks, 0–2 ruins. Sparser, so the lanes stay
  clean and the fights stay readable.
- **High ground:** bare and wind-scoured — 1–3 rocks, occasional dead tree, scoured rock outcrops.
  The visual contrast between bare high ground and dense low jungle is what makes elevation *read*.
- **River banks:** reeds, wet stones, sand/silt deposits, driftwood.

**Variation law:** every scattered prop instance gets seeded variation in **scale (±30%), rotation
(full 360°), lean (±12°), and one of ≥3 color-tint steps** drawn from its family. A visible grid,
a visible repeat, or two identical adjacent instances is a defect a reviewer files.

**Landmarks.** The map needs 4–6 unique, hand-placed set pieces that make locations nameable — a
collapsed arch over the river, a fallen colossus half-buried in the jungle, a ring of standing
stones on the high ground. Players navigate by landmarks; identical terrain everywhere is why
procedural worlds feel machine-made.

**Everything static bakes.** Scatter is merged by material into batched geometry, or instanced. The
draw-call budget is not optional (§ `GRAPHICS_CONTRACT.md` §5) and it is what lets us afford this
density at all.

## 9. FX & game feel

FX are a first-class visual workstream, not a garnish. Every one has the same four-part structure:
**anticipation → strike → aftermath → decal.**

- **Ability casts:** a wind-up tell at the caster (ring, gathering motes, a stance change), a bright
  emissive core at the strike, a colored bloom, radial debris/sparks, and a **ground decal that
  outlives the effect** by 1–3s. The decal is what makes an ability feel like it *happened to the
  world*.
- **Impacts:** directional spark cone along the hit normal, a brief emissive flash on the struck
  unit, and a hit-pause of 40–70ms on heavy hits.
- **Deaths:** each unit family gets its **own** death — heroes collapse and leave a fading soul
  wisp; creeps ragdoll-topple and dissolve into motes; structures crumble in stages with dust
  plumes and settle into rubble that **stays on the map**. The current single shared wraith ghost
  for every death is a defect to be removed.
- **Projectiles:** a lit core with a tapered trail and a subtle light-scatter, oriented to flight.
- **Ambient life:** drifting pollen/embers by day, fireflies and mist by night, wind moving the
  canopy, water flowing in the river, banners and cloth in motion. **A still frame of a MOBA should
  never look still** — this is cheap and it is a large part of what "shipped" means.
- **Screen feel:** shake on heavy impacts only, scaled and rare. A brief chromatic/vignette pulse on
  taking heavy damage.

Particle budget rises from the current 240 to whatever the perf budget in `GRAPHICS_CONTRACT.md` §5
allows, spent through pooled instanced meshes. 240 total particles cannot express any of the above.

## 10. Fog of war

The fog is a large fraction of every frame and currently reads as a flat grey lid. It must read as
**atmosphere, not a UI overlay**: soft-edged, subtly animated drifting mist over unexplored ground,
darkening-with-desaturation over explored-but-not-visible ground rather than flat greying, and a
soft, slightly warm visibility falloff at the edge of vision. Team units stay readable against
fog-darkened terrain — that ladder assertion still binds.

## 10a. Baseline defects observed on pixels (fix these by name)

Found by the orchestrator looking at rendered captures of the current build, not by reading code.
Each is a specific, named thing to kill — a reviewer checks them off.

1. **The ground mottling reads as visible rectangles.** The decal quads scattered over the moss are
   plainly rectangular and axis-ish aligned at gameplay zoom, so the ground looks patched rather than
   varied. Ground variation must come from the surface's own noise/normal maps and from irregular,
   rotated, soft-edged forms — never from visible flat quads laid on the plane. (R_TERRAIN)
2. **Shadows are huge soft blobs with no definition.** A tower casts a shapeless smear. This is the
   whole-map shadow frustum at 2048 spending nearly all its resolution on empty ground. The
   view-fitted, texel-snapped frustum at 4096 in §4 is the fix, and its success criterion is concrete:
   a tower's shadow must show its cornice and brazier as distinct forms. (R_SCENE)
3. **The lane reads as beige tape laid on grass.** Its edge is a pure colour step with no geometry.
   Lanes need real edge treatment — a kerb with thickness, worn/broken margins where traffic spills
   onto the verge, embedded flagstones, and dirt bleeding into the moss — so the lane looks *built
   into* the ground rather than painted onto it. (R_MAPMESH)
4. **Trees are the textbook lollipop**: one cylinder plus one green icosahedron. §7's six archetypes
   with root flare, branch levels and layered canopy shells exist to end this. (R_VEG)
5. **The fog-of-war edge is a hard flat darkening** that reads as a UI lid dropped over the world,
   which §10 already calls out. Confirmed on pixels. (R_FOG)

The palette, for the record, is **not** a defect — the moss/sand/team-colour relationships read well
and are pleasant on screen. The failure is entirely form, detail and light, which is where the
budget goes.

## 11. The hard bans

Violations a reviewer files without discussion:

- Ad-hoc hex literals in implementer code.
- `new THREE.MeshStandardMaterial` (or any material constructor) outside the frozen kit factory.
- `MeshBasicMaterial` / `MeshLambertMaterial` / `MeshPhongMaterial` on a sun-lit surface.
- A PBR material with `scene.environment` unset — plastic-look, and the classic failure of this
  exact conversion.
- Any image asset, `TextureLoader`, font file, or network-fetched resource.
- `Math.random` anywhere under `games/rift/` — all variation comes from the seeded RNG in the kit, or
  captures will not be reproducible between judge rounds and the whole loop breaks.
- Disabling shadows, AO, or bloom for performance.
- `ACESFilmicToneMapping` — measured and rejected in an earlier round (see §6 item 5).
- A `HemisphereLight` alongside `scene.environment` — double-counted fill.
- Setting `texture.repeat` by hand, or any per-object texture scale (see the UV law).
- Per-frame allocation in the render loop.
- A lollipop tree, a Platonic-solid rock, or a flat untextured ground plane.
